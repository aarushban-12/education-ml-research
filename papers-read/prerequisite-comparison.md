### Comparison of Existing Prerequisite Knowledge Tracing Research and Ability-Stratified Reliability

Recent research has increasingly focused on making knowledge tracing models more interpretable by explicitly modeling prerequisite relationships and concept dependencies. Earlier prerequisite-oriented models, such as Prerequisite-Driven Knowledge Tracing (PDKT), incorporate prerequisite graphs or concept relationships to improve student modeling and identify missing prerequisite knowledge. More recent interpretable models, including PSI-KT and other explainable knowledge tracing approaches, combine probabilistic modeling, attention mechanisms, or hierarchical concept representations to produce explanations alongside accurate predictions. Their primary objective is to improve prerequisite identification and provide educators with more interpretable descriptions of student knowledge.

After reviewing these papers, I found that prerequisite-aware knowledge tracing has become a well-developed research direction. Many recent models explicitly represent prerequisite structures, question difficulty, concept relationships, or causal dependencies rather than focusing only on next-question prediction.

My research addresses a different question. Instead of proposing another prerequisite-aware architecture, I investigate whether existing prerequisite-aware knowledge tracing models are equally reliable across students with different prior ability levels. Current papers typically report overall performance metrics such as AUC or accuracy, but these aggregate metrics may conceal important differences between beginner and advanced learners. By stratifying students according to estimated ability and evaluating prerequisite-gap identification separately within each group, my research aims to determine when these models can be trusted and whether their educational recommendations remain consistent across diverse learner populations.
